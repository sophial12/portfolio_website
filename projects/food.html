Food: The Boundaries Between Raw and Ripe

17” x 14” x 4.5”, Acrylic on fabric

Delicious from far away, disgusting up close. This piece explores the boundaries between raw and ripe food, using acrylic paint on fabric to create a visually appealing yet unsettling image. The juxtaposition of vibrant colors and textures invites viewers to question their perceptions of food and the fine line between nourishment and decay.

Role
Artist
Year

Project Details

The brief, approach, and results

Brief

Charcuterie boards are defined as trays with a variety of sweet and savory bites. In this piece, I explored the limits to which savory and sweet foods can be paired together. This charcuterie board looks appetizing from afar, but when taking a closer look, the charcuterie board is actually composed of raw fish, lotus root, lychee, grapefruit, and tea leaves, most of which are unappealing on their own. Paying homage to the artistic practice of trompe l’oiel, the objects are painted to appear real, and the physically sculpted fish guts and found object fork add to the illusion by breaking the 2-dimensional picture plane.

Outcome

Received national recognition from the Scholastic Art & Writing Awards with a Silver Medal award in the Painting category.
